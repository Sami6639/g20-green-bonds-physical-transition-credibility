## P01 — Scientific problem and candidate gap

### Scientific problem

The rapid growth of labelled green finance does not by itself establish that national electricity systems are moving away from fossil generation. Green-bond issuance can coexist with renewable expansion, continued fossil growth, coal-to-gas or coal-to-oil offsetting, or an absence of absolute fossil contraction. A credible assessment therefore requires observable physical margins that become progressively more demanding than renewable growth alone.

### Broad hypothesis rejected

The broad question of whether green bonds increase renewable energy, reduce fossil-energy use, or lower emissions is already populated by multi-country panel, firm-level and market studies. It cannot support a defensible novelty claim for this project.

### Narrow candidate gap

The candidate contribution is a multi-margin physical-alignment architecture for G20 electricity systems. It will distinguish, without collapsing the outcomes into an arbitrary weighted index:

1. renewable-electricity addition;
2. coal-power contraction;
3. absolute total-fossil-power contraction;
4. coal contraction that is not offset by higher gas or oil generation; and
5. persistence of the more demanding physical states, if the pre-specified design and sample support it.

The study will evaluate how national green-bond-market development aligns with these separate physical outcomes. It will not treat green-bond issuance as proof of domestic project finance, and it will not interpret contemporaneous or lagged associations as causal effects.

### Separation from the three completed manuscripts

The new study will not reuse any of the following as its primary contribution:

1. cross-boundary signal validity for durable economy-wide fossil displacement;
2. country-count versus TWh-weighted breadth, depth and contribution concentration; or
3. renewable-addition versus fossil-displacement state transitions, persistence and reversibility.

Their measurement lessons remain binding: absolute quantities must accompany shares; indicator boundaries must be explicit; persistence claims require a pre-specified temporal rule; and country-level clustered inference with 19 clusters must be treated cautiously.

### Separation from the frozen coal-offsetting candidate

The earlier coal-offsetting project is centred on the physical decomposition of coal-contraction events. In the present project, within-fossil offsetting can enter only as one observable credibility margin inside a finance–physical-alignment question. It cannot be presented as a second primary novelty claim or reproduced as a stand-alone paper within this manuscript.

### Critical boundary and weakest evidence link

The IMF series assigns issuance by the issuer's country of incorporation. It does not identify the destination country, electricity-sector share or project-level use of proceeds. Consequently, country-level issuance cannot be assumed to finance domestic electricity assets. The defensible estimand is national financial–physical **alignment**, not financing effectiveness, additionality or project impact.

This boundary must appear in the title logic, research question, methods, discussion and conclusion. Terms such as `impact`, `effect`, `delivery by green bonds`, `financed fossil exit`, and `caused transition` are prohibited unless a future design introduces evidence that can identify them.

### Provisional measurement architecture

The project should use a vector or ladder of separately reported physical margins rather than a researcher-weighted credibility score. Candidate margins may be nested for interpretation, but each underlying indicator and denominator must remain visible. The financial dimension may include recorded issuance activation, annual issuance scale and cumulative market development; the exact primary measure will be selected only at P03–P05, before analysis of substantive relationships.

## P03 — Research Question Architecture

### Candidate questions evaluated

1. **Jurisdiction-level alignment:** Across G20 electricity systems, to what extent is recorded green-bond issuance aligned with distinct annual physical transition outcomes: renewable addition, coal contraction, absolute fossil contraction, and coal contraction without gas- or oil-based offsetting?
2. **Lagged prediction:** Does greater green-bond issuance intensity predict more stringent physical transition outcomes in the following year?
3. **Market-entry transition:** Do countries move more frequently into fossil-contraction states after their first recorded green-bond issuance?

Candidate 2 was not selected because a one-year lag has no uniquely defensible project-delivery interpretation and would invite an effect claim unsupported by the country-of-incorporation data. Candidate 3 was not selected because market entry is strongly patterned by global time and country selection; an event-study form could be mistaken for causal identification without a credible untreated counterfactual.

### Locked Primary RQ

> Across G20 electricity systems, to what extent is recorded green-bond issuance aligned with distinct annual physical transition outcomes: renewable addition, coal contraction, absolute fossil contraction, and coal contraction without gas- or oil-based offsetting?

### Locked sub-questions

1. How frequently does recorded green-bond issuance coexist with each physical outcome, and how large is the difference between renewable-addition and absolute-fossil-contraction frequencies?
2. After accounting for stable country characteristics and common year shocks, how does green-bond issuance intensity relate to the probability of absolute fossil contraction, and how does that association compare with the other physical margins?
3. Are the descriptive and associational conclusions sensitive to material-change thresholds, the treatment of years with no recorded issuance, the scale denominator, sample period, or any single country?

### Pre-specified hypotheses

- **H1:** Higher green-bond issuance intensity is positively associated with the annual probability of renewable-electricity addition.
- **H2:** If green-bond-market development is physically aligned with fossil exit, higher issuance intensity is positively associated with the annual probability of absolute fossil-power contraction.
- **H3:** Among coal-reliant country-years, higher issuance intensity is positively associated with coal contraction without a contemporaneous increase in combined gas- and oil-fired generation.

The hypotheses concern alignment, not treatment effects. A null result, a renewable-only association, or a weaker association for fossil contraction than for renewable addition would remain substantively meaningful because each would delimit what labelled financial growth reveals about physical system change.

### Novelty traceability

- **Verified gap:** Existing studies relate green bonds to emissions, renewable energy, broad fossil consumption, policy diversity or corporate outcomes, but do not establish the proposed set of annual electricity-generation margins as a jurisdiction-level alignment architecture.
- **Closest competitors:** Ullah et al. (2026), Nguyen et al. (2025), Huang et al. (2023), Mertzanis (2023), Zhou et al. (2026), and BIS (2025).
- **Exact difference:** The present design keeps renewable addition, total fossil contraction and within-fossil offsetting analytically separate, uses absolute generation changes, and treats divergence across those margins as the result rather than assuming renewable growth is equivalent to fossil exit.
- **Why it matters:** Financial labels and market volumes can expand while the electricity system remains addition-dominant. Distinguishing physical margins makes that boundary observable without attributing domestic projects or causal effects to the bonds.

## P04 — Critical-Link Validation

### Highest-risk link

The weakest link is the bridge from green bonds assigned to an issuer's country of incorporation to physical changes in that country's electricity system. The data do not establish destination, sector allocation, project timing or additionality.

### Evidence map and link classification

1. **Recorded issuance measures activity attributed to an issuer jurisdiction:** Strong evidence for the financial-market location represented in the IMF/LSEG aggregation.
2. **Issuer jurisdiction equals destination of proceeds:** Weak and not defensible as a general assumption.
3. **Green-bond proceeds are allocated to domestic electricity assets:** Not observed and not defensible with the selected aggregate data.
4. **Annual OWID generation changes represent physical national electricity outcomes:** Strong for the defined generation boundary, subject to source revisions and annual measurement limits.
5. **Jurisdiction-level co-movement can be interpreted as financial–physical alignment:** Usable with uncertainty when country and common-year structure are controlled and causal language is excluded.
6. **Jurisdiction-level alignment identifies financing effectiveness or additionality:** Not defensible.

### Recommended representation

- Use green-bond issuance as a jurisdiction-level financial signal, not a project-level treatment.
- Use separately reported physical binary outcomes and absolute TWh changes; do not construct a researcher-weighted credibility index.
- Treat contemporaneous alignment as primary. A one-year lag may appear only as a sensitivity analysis and cannot be interpreted as a project-delivery lag.
- Use `no recorded issuance` rather than `verified zero issuance` in data descriptions.
- Define `physical transition credibility` narrowly as observable consistency between the financial signal and increasingly demanding power-system outcomes. It is not a bond rating, certification assessment or causal impact measure.

### False-result risks and controls

| Risk | Required control |
|---|---|
| Global growth in green bonds and common decarbonisation trends | Year fixed effects and period sensitivity |
| Stable cross-country differences in market depth and power mix | Country fixed effects |
| China, the United States or another large market driving results | Leave-one-country-out diagnostics |
| Trivial annual movements generating directional states | 0.10% and 0.25% prior-year total-generation materiality thresholds |
| Missing-versus-zero ambiguity in IMF cells | `No recorded issuance` definition; post-entry and positive-issuance sensitivities |
| Country size driving issuance volumes | GDP-normalised primary intensity; active-issuance indicator and per-capita sensitivity |
| Four outcome families inflating significance claims | One primary endpoint; secondary-family multiplicity adjustment |
| Only 19 country clusters | CRV3 cluster-jackknife primary inference and wild-cluster bootstrap-t sensitivity |
| Reverse causality and omitted time-varying policy factors | Explicit associational interpretation; no causal mechanism claim |
| Reuse of prior-paper contributions | No persistence model, transition matrix, breadth/depth concentration architecture or boundary-validity test as a headline contribution |

### G4 decision

- **Decision:** GO WITH CONSTRAINTS
- **Binding constraint:** The study may estimate jurisdiction-level alignment only. Any claim about financed domestic projects, effectiveness, additionality or causality triggers STOP/change control.

## P05 — Methodological Architecture Lock

### Locked functional unit and primary endpoint

- **Functional unit:** Country-year.
- **Primary physical endpoint:** Annual absolute total-fossil-electricity contraction, measured from TWh generation.
- **Benchmark endpoint:** Annual renewable-electricity addition.
- **Secondary physical endpoints:** Coal-electricity contraction and coal contraction without a contemporaneous increase in combined gas- and oil-fired generation.
- **Main financial exposure:** Annual recorded green-bond issuance as a share of nominal GDP, expressed in basis points of GDP.
- **Supporting exposure:** Indicator for a positive amount recorded in the IMF/LSEG series.

### Locked physical definitions

Let `ΔX_it = X_it - X_i,t-1`, where generation is measured in TWh. Under the strict-sign baseline:

- `Renewable addition = 1[ΔRenewables_it > 0]`.
- `Coal contraction = 1[ΔCoal_it < 0]`, evaluated only where prior-year coal generation is positive.
- `Absolute fossil contraction = 1[ΔFossil_it < 0]`.
- `Unoffset coal contraction = 1[ΔCoal_it < 0 and ΔGas_it + ΔOil_it <= 0]`, evaluated only in coal-eligible country-years.

Materiality sensitivities replace the zero boundary for the focal generation change with 0.10% and 0.25% of prior-year total electricity generation. The no-offset condition retains the transparent combined gas-plus-oil sign rule.

### Locked financial definitions

- Annual issuance is the IMF/LSEG amount attributed to the issuer's country of incorporation, in current USD.
- Primary intensity equals `10,000 × issuance_USD / nominal_GDP_USD` and is interpreted as basis points of GDP.
- A blank annual IMF cell is coded as zero only for computation of `no recorded issuance`; it is never described as proof that no qualifying bond existed outside the database.
- Years before a country's first positive recorded amount are separated in sensitivity analysis from post-entry years with no recorded amount.
- Cumulative issuance may be descriptive but is not a substitute for country-level outstanding amounts and is excluded from the primary model.

### Sample boundary

- **Countries:** Argentina, Australia, Brazil, Canada, China, France, Germany, India, Indonesia, Italy, Japan, Mexico, Russia, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, South Korea, Türkiye, the United Kingdom and the United States.
- **Excluded as a separate observation:** The European Union, to avoid overlap with member countries.
- **Main years:** 2006–2024; 2005 is used only to construct first differences.
- **Main panel:** 361 country-years before endpoint-specific eligibility restrictions.
- **Coal endpoints:** Restricted to country-years with positive prior-year coal generation; countries without exposure remain in non-coal outcomes.

### Main empirical architecture

1. Report the annual and country distribution of the financial signal without interpreting it as project allocation.
2. Report the frequency and absolute-TWh magnitude of each physical outcome for all country-years and for positive-recorded-issuance years.
3. Treat absolute fossil contraction as the single primary endpoint.
4. Estimate a country- and year-fixed-effects linear probability model for the association between issuance intensity and the primary endpoint.
5. Estimate identically specified models for the benchmark and secondary endpoints; label them comparative/secondary.
6. Use CRV3 cluster-jackknife covariance by country with finite-cluster `t(G-1)` reference inference as the primary reported covariance method.
7. Report wild-cluster bootstrap-t inference as a pre-specified sensitivity.
8. Report estimates, confidence intervals, economically interpretable probability changes and the non-causal boundary; do not promote isolated p-values.

### Sensitivity architecture

- strict sign, 0.10% and 0.25% material-change definitions;
- active-issuance indicator in place of intensity;
- one-year-lagged issuance intensity as a non-causal timing sensitivity;
- per-capita issuance as a scale sensitivity;
- post-2015 period sensitivity;
- exclusion of pre-entry blank years;
- leave-one-country-out estimates;
- country-resampling descriptive intervals with a fixed seed;
- Holm adjustment across the three secondary endpoint tests.

### Excluded extensions

- project-level use-of-proceeds claims;
- causal event study, difference-in-differences, instrumental variables or treatment-effect language;
- a weighted `credibility score` selected by the researcher;
- latent Markov regimes, transition matrices or spell/persistence modelling;
- country rankings presented as bond-quality ratings;
- fuel-price, regulatory or institutional mechanism searches added after results;
- ad hoc regional subgroups or post-result lag selection.

### Minimum viable scientific model and complexity audit

The locked minimum is one transparent descriptive alignment profile plus one fixed-effects model for the primary endpoint and identically specified comparative models. Every retained component either defines the financial–physical boundary, addresses the panel structure or tests a pre-specified measurement risk. Methods that would add complexity without identifying the issuer-to-project bridge are excluded.
